This page lists what DanyWhere can do, in short. Where a chapter explains a part in depth, the section links to it.

## Ending a dictation

- **Decide at the end what happens to a dictation.** A recording starts with a start shortcut and ends with an end shortcut of your choice. Each end shortcut has a key combination of its own and says what follows the text and whether earlier dictations go along as context.
- **Four lists of the same kind:** start, hold, instruction and end shortcuts, as many of each as you like. What a combination does follows from the lists it stands in, so one combination can start with a tap and record while held.
- **Six end shortcuts on a Mac, in a new installation,** by one rule: ⇧ (Shift) picks the smaller variant, ↩ (Return) sends, and ⌥ (Option) keeps it from sending. <kbd>⌃ Space</kbd> ends with a space, <kbd>↩</kbd> presses Enter to send a chat message, and <kbd>⌥↩</kbd> ends with a new paragraph.
- **On Windows and Linux,** the combinations of the two hold shortcuts end the recording.
- **Hold or tap.** Hold a hold shortcut while you speak, or tap a start shortcut to start and tap again to stop. A short tap on a key that only holds does nothing.
- **Text and a key press after the dictation.** An end shortcut can append text, such as a space or a new line, and then press a key such as Enter.

[Start, hold, instruction and end shortcuts](../how-it-works/start-and-end-shortcuts/) explains all of this.

## The spoken instruction

- **Say what should happen to the text, right after it.** During a recording, an instruction shortcut (<kbd>⇥</kbd> on a Mac, <kbd>Tab</kbd> on Windows) switches from the text to an instruction, such as “translate this into Polish” or “answer the question”. The language model applies it on top of the profile's prompts, for this one dictation.
- **Tap or hold.** Tap the instruction shortcut and end with any end shortcut, or hold it while you speak and let go.
- **No waiting.** The text is transcribed while you're still saying the instruction.
- **An instruction without text** writes something from scratch, such as a polite reply.
- **The text stays text.** Only what you say after the key counts as an instruction, and later dictations get the result as context, never the instruction.
- **Kept in the History** with its audio: **Re-transcribe** hears both parts again, and the wand works with or without the instruction.

[The spoken instruction](../how-it-works/spoken-instruction/) shows how to use it.

## Defaults and profiles

- **Defaults, and profiles that change only what they need.** Every setting that concerns a dictation has a default, from the speech model, languages and post-processing to the shortcuts, microphone, sounds and word list. A profile follows the defaults and keeps its own value only where you set one. Each setting shows which value applies, and the defaults show which profiles differ.
- **Settings of the app itself,** such as the interface language, autostart and updates, are the same for every profile.
- **You choose what dictations use** in **In Use**, at the top of the sidebar, or in the tray.
- **A profile per app.** Give a profile the apps it is for, for example one for your terminal and another for your chat. The app in front when the recording ends decides. When the text later goes into another app, DanyWhere redoes only what that app's profile does differently. **Choose Profile by Application** switches this off and on, also with <kbd>⌃⇧A</kbd> on a Mac and <kbd>Alt+Shift+A</kbd> on Windows. On Windows you type the program names; on Linux DanyWhere can't tell the app in front yet.
- **A profile to take along.** Export a profile to a file and import it elsewhere. Its API key is encrypted with a password you choose.

[Defaults and profiles](../how-it-works/defaults-and-profiles/) shows how the two work together.

## Post-processing

- **Never, with an instruction shortcut, or always.** Each profile says when its dictations are post-processed: never, so nothing leaves your computer; only after an instruction shortcut, as a new installation does; or every time.
- **A language model works on your text instead of answering it.** DanyWhere tells the model itself that it gets a transcript to work on, not a question, that the language stays, and that it corrects misheard words where the context makes clear what was said. Your prompt only says what to do with the text. **Effort**, next to the model, sets how much the model thinks.
- **Several prompts in one go.** The prompts are one library for all profiles. Each profile switches on the ones it uses and puts them in order. They go to the model in one request and apply in turn.
- **Your word lists as known terms.** Post-processing gets the word lists in effect, so it writes a misheard name as listed, with every speech model.
- **Earlier dictations as context.** With **Send Context**, an end, hold or instruction shortcut sends your earlier dictations from the same window along, so the model knows what the conversation is about.
- **On this computer or in the cloud, and you see which.** Next to the provider, the settings say whether post-processing stays on this computer or goes to the cloud. With a model served on this computer, for example through Ollama or LM Studio, DanyWhere loads it as soon as you start speaking, so its cold start doesn't add to the wait.

## History

- **Every edit kept as a version.** Each entry is a stack of versions with its result on top. Post-processing, your own edit and transcribing again add a version on top and delete nothing, and you can move and delete versions.
- **Edit and post-process later.** Click into the text to edit it. The wand post-processes the version you see, with the profile's prompts or any single prompt, long after the dictation too. Each entry names the speech model that transcribed it.
- **Search the whole History,** every version included, with match case, whole words and regular expressions, and filter by app, window title or file name.
- **A long History that stays small.** Recordings are stored compressed, about 12 times smaller than uncompressed audio. A storage limit, 500 MB by default or about 54 hours of dictation, decides how many recordings stay. When old recordings go, their transcripts stay, and a star keeps a recording for good.

## Inserting and copying

- **Inserting without the clipboard on a Mac.** DanyWhere has an input method of its own. Once you add its input source, starting from **Application Settings › Accessibility › Insert Without the Clipboard**, a dictation goes straight into the text field, without the clipboard.
- **The last transcript, once more.** **Paste Last Transcript** puts the most recent transcript where the cursor is now, for example after a dictation went nowhere because no text field had focus: <kbd>⌃⇧V</kbd> on a Mac, <kbd>Alt+Shift+V</kbd> on Windows and Linux. **Copy Last Transcript** puts it on the clipboard: <kbd>⌃⇧C</kbd>, or <kbd>Alt+Shift+C</kbd>.
- **The overlay on the screen you work on,** the one with the window in front, on macOS and Windows.

## Without Accessibility on a Mac

- **DanyWhere works where this permission is blocked,** as on many managed Macs. Choose **Continue without Accessibility** during setup. With DanyWhere's input source added, end shortcuts insert the text right away. Without it, a dictation waits in DanyWhere until you insert it with <kbd>⌃⇧V</kbd>.
- **DanyWhere can't press keys without the permission.** A new installation leaves out the two end shortcuts that press a key, and you press Enter yourself.
- **Window titles from the apps themselves.** DanyWhere asks each app that can tell the title of its front window, such as browsers, Terminal, Mail, Notes, TextEdit and Microsoft Office, once you allow it for that app. Context from earlier dictations and the History's window filter need the title.
- **Shortcuts with the fn key** are not available without the permission.

## Speech models and word lists

- **Speech recognition on your own computer,** with a small selection of models fast enough for dictation on a laptop: Parakeet Ultra (25 European languages), Whisper large-v3-turbo (100 languages), Canary 1B v2 (translates into English), Parakeet unified EN 0.6B (English, with a live preview), Whisper small and Canary 180M flash.
- **A model for your languages from the start.** Setup recommends one for the languages you speak and downloads it while you grant the permissions. Every file is checked against its checksum.
- **Word lists per topic.** The **Word List** of the defaults applies in every profile, and a profile can add **Additional Words** of its own. Named additional lists, for example one per project, are switched on one at a time in the settings, in the tray or with <kbd>⌃⇧L</kbd> (<kbd>Alt+Shift+L</kbd> on Windows and Linux). The Whisper models use the word lists while they recognize speech.
- **Names checked by the language model.** Canary and Parakeet don't use the word lists themselves. With **Check Names with the LLM, Also Without Post-Processing**, DanyWhere asks your post-processing provider whether a word that resembles a list entry means it, and replaces only what the provider confirms.

## More

- **A sound when everything is done,** post-processing included, so you know when to look back. The four sounds rise from start to instruction to stop to done.
- **Key combinations as your system writes them,** <kbd>⌃⇧ Space</kbd> on a Mac and <kbd>Ctrl+Shift+Space</kbd> on Windows. In a combination with a main key, the left and the right modifier keys count alike.
- **What's new after an update,** in English or German.
- **This manual,** in the app under **Manual** and on the web, in English, German, Spanish and French.
