You press a key combination, say what you want to write, and end the recording. A moment later the text is in the app you're working in: as you said it or polished by a language model, followed by a space or a new paragraph, or already sent. This chapter follows one dictation along that way. Once you know the steps, you know where to change something when the result isn't what you wanted.

## Start the recording

Press a start shortcut: <kbd>⌃ Space</kbd> on a Mac, <kbd>Ctrl+Space</kbd> on Windows, <kbd>Ctrl+Alt+Space</kbd> on Linux. DanyWhere starts listening at once. On a Mac and on Windows, a small overlay at the bottom of the screen shows that it's recording, and if you've switched on **Audio Feedback**, a short sound tells you too.

You can use the combination in two ways: hold it while you speak and let go when you're done, or tap it and talk for as long as you like. That's because a new installation has it twice, under **Start Shortcuts** and under **Hold Shortcuts**.

## End it the way you want the text

How you end the recording decides what follows the text. There are three ways:

- **Let go** of a hold shortcut you were holding. The recording ends the way that hold shortcut says.
- **Tap an end shortcut.** Each one says what comes after the text and whether earlier dictations go along as context. On a Mac, for example, <kbd>↩</kbd> ends the recording and presses Enter after the text, which sends a chat message; <kbd>⌥↩</kbd> starts a new paragraph instead.
- **Tap the start shortcut again.** This ends the recording when an end shortcut has the same combination. On a Mac, <kbd>⌃ Space</kbd> again puts a space after the text, ready for the next sentence.

Before you end, an instruction shortcut lets you say what a language model should do with the text: tap <kbd>⇥</kbd> on a Mac, say “translate this into Polish”, and end as always. [The spoken instruction](../spoken-instruction/) shows how.

For an app where inserting doesn't work, end with **Copy Last Transcript** instead: <kbd>⌃⇧C</kbd> on a Mac, <kbd>Alt+Shift+C</kbd> on Windows and Linux. It ends the recording like tapping the start shortcut again, but puts the text on the clipboard instead of inserting it.

Changed your mind? <kbd>Esc</kbd> cancels the recording on a Mac and on Windows. Nothing is transcribed, inserted or kept.

End shortcuts are the heart of DanyWhere, and [Start, hold, instruction and end shortcuts](../start-and-end-shortcuts/) covers them all.

## Which profile applies

The settings that shape a dictation are chosen when the recording ends. If **Choose Profile by Application** is on and a profile lists the app in front at that moment, that profile applies. Otherwise the one chosen under **In Use** at the top of the sidebar applies: the **Defaults** or a profile. A profile follows the defaults wherever it has no **Own value**, so it only holds what should differ, such as another speech model for your terminal. Because the choice is made at the end, you can start a dictation anywhere and switch to the app the text belongs in before you end it. [Defaults and profiles](../defaults-and-profiles/) shows how profiles work.

## Transcription

As soon as the recording ends, the speech model turns it into text. This happens on your computer; the recording doesn't leave it. The overlay shows **Transcribing...**.

Names and technical terms come out right more often when they're on a word list. Whisper models use the lists while they recognize speech, and with every model, post-processing gets them as known terms.

## Post-processing

If you pressed an instruction shortcut during the recording, or the profile post-processes every dictation, a language model works on the text before it arrives. It gets the prompts marked **Active** on the page **Post Process**, in their order, and your spoken instruction last. DanyWhere puts a frame around them that tells the model it has a transcript to work on, not a question to answer, and that the text keeps its language, unless your instruction asks for something else. The overlay shows **Processing...**.

Which of the two applies is set by **Post Processing**, under **Defaults › Advanced** or in the profile: **Never**, **With Instruction Shortcut** (the factory setting), or **Always**, for every dictation. Whether the text goes to a provider in the cloud or to a model on your own computer depends on the provider you choose on the page **Post Process**, and the page says which. [Setting up post-processing](../post-processing/) shows how a few questions find one for you.

With **Send Context**, earlier dictations from the same window go along, so the model knows what you've been writing about: by default up to five from the last hour. The History marks every entry that went out this way.

If post-processing fails, say because the provider can't be reached, you still get your words. DanyWhere inserts the transcript as it is, and a notice, **Pasted Without Post-Processing**, tells you why.

## Append

Next, the end's **Append** comes after the text: a space, a line break or any text you like, and if you want, a key press such as Enter. The text is appended however the dictation arrives. The key press only follows when DanyWhere pastes the text itself. That's how one end shortcut continues your sentence and another sends a chat message right away.

## Insert

Now the text goes where your cursor is.

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="macOS">

With the **Accessibility** permission, DanyWhere puts the text on the clipboard, presses <kbd>⌘V</kbd> for you and then puts back what was on the clipboard before. The key press from **Append** follows, if there is one.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows">

DanyWhere puts the text on the clipboard, presses <kbd>Ctrl+V</kbd> for you and then puts back what was on the clipboard before. The key press from **Append** follows, if there is one. **Paste Method** under **Defaults › Advanced** offers other ways, such as typing the text directly.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Linux">

DanyWhere pastes with <kbd>Shift+Insert</kbd>, which works in terminals as well as in other apps, and then puts back what was on the clipboard before. The key press from **Append** follows, if there is one. **Paste Method** under **Defaults › Advanced** offers other ways, including a script of your own.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::tip[The text went nowhere?]
If no text field had the focus, nothing is lost. Put the cursor where the text belongs and press **Paste Last Transcript**: <kbd>⌃⇧V</kbd> on a Mac, <kbd>Alt+Shift+V</kbd> on Windows and Linux.
:::

:::caution[On a Mac without Accessibility]
Where the Accessibility permission is blocked, as on many company Macs, DanyWhere can't paste with <kbd>⌘V</kbd>. It inserts the text through its own input method instead, once you add its input source, starting from **Application Settings › Accessibility › Insert Without the Clipboard**. Without that input source, the text waits in DanyWhere until you insert it with **Paste Last Transcript** (<kbd>⌃⇧V</kbd>) where it belongs; **Copy Last Transcript** (<kbd>⌃⇧C</kbd>) puts it on the clipboard instead. Key presses from **Append** aren't sent either way.
:::

## History

Every dictation lands in the **History**: the recording, the transcription, the spoken instruction if there was one, and, if it was post-processed, the polished text as a second version on top. DanyWhere saves it before inserting, so nothing is lost when pasting goes wrong. You can edit an entry or post-process it again later; each change adds a new version on top and keeps the ones below.

## The done sound

Out of the box, four sounds mark the way, each higher than the one before: one when the recording starts, one when an instruction shortcut switches to the spoken instruction, one when the recording stops, and the highest once everything is done, post-processing included. When a language model takes a few seconds and you've looked elsewhere, that last sound tells you the text is ready. Cancelling with <kbd>Esc</kbd> plays a sound lower than all of them. You'll find **Audio Feedback**, which turns them all off, and **Sound When Done**, which turns off just the last sound, under **Defaults › General**.
