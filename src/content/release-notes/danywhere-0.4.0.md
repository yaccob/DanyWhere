## A user manual

DanyWhere now has a manual: **Manual** in the sidebar, between History and About. Its first three chapters explain a dictation from start to end, start and end shortcuts, and defaults and profiles, with diagrams and pictures of the settings.

- It's in English, German, Spanish and French, in the interface language where it has one and otherwise in English.
- The **?** next to **End Shortcuts** and on the profile pages opens the chapter that belongs there.
- The same manual is on the web, at [yaccob.github.io/DanyWhere](https://yaccob.github.io/DanyWhere/).

## Twelve end shortcuts on a new Mac

A new installation on macOS starts with twelve end shortcuts that follow one rule: ⌥ post-processes, ⇧ picks the smaller variant, and ⌃ with ↩ presses a key instead of appending text.

- ⌃Space ends with a space, ⌃⇧Space with nothing after the text, ↩ with a new paragraph, ⇧↩ with a new line, ⌃↩ presses Enter to send a chat message, ⌃⇧↩ presses Shift+Enter.
- The same six with ⌥, from ⌥⌃Space to ⌥⌃⇧↩, post-process the text and send earlier dictations from the same window along.
- Letting go of a held start shortcut appends a space; with the **Second Start Shortcut** the text is post-processed as well.
- Their names are written once, in your language at the first start, and you can rename them.
- If you used DanyWhere before, your end shortcuts stay as they are. To use the new ones, add them under **Defaults › General › End Shortcuts**; the manual's chapter on start and end shortcuts lists them.

## Also new

- On macOS, key combinations write ⌥ before ⌃ (⌥⌃↩), so a post-processing end stands out by its first symbol.
- The three sounds rise: the start sound is the lowest, the stop sound higher and the sound when everything is done the highest, so you hear how far a dictation has got.
- **About** says where DanyWhere comes from: it grew out of Handy, and thanks its makers.
